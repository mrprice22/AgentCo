# AgentCo — Service Management (SLAs, Operations & Continuity)

**Design document v0.1**
**Scope:** How AgentCo is *operated* as a service to its one customer, the human. Covers:
- service levels in three directions: what the system promises the human, what the human is asked for, and what the internal components promise each other
- what happens when the human doesn't answer
- what happens when a tier, supplier, or the host fails
- incident and problem management, monitoring and alerting
- capacity and performance, backup and recovery, supplier operations
- vulnerability remediation, status reporting, and continual improvement

Maps to ITIL 4 service management practices, NIST SP 800-53 CP/IR/SI families, SSDF RV, and AI RMF MANAGE.

---

## 1. AgentCo as a service

### 1.1 Service catalog

The human is simultaneously owner and only customer (governance design §2). Writing the services down makes it possible to say what "working" means for each one:

| Service | What the human gets | Primary components |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — Product delivery** | Working software built from their idea, released on approval | Engine, T0–T2 agents, CI, product repos |
| **S2 — Decision support** | Well-framed questions only when needed; everything else decided and logged | Exec Dir, Client Comms (T3), human relay, inbox |
| **S3 — Transparency** | Status, forecasts, spend, decisions-without-you, compliance evidence | Monitor, Client Comms, audit log |
| **S4 — Platform operations** | The system itself stays up, safe, within budget, and recoverable | Driver, router, stores, suppliers, host |

### 1.2 Three layers of service level

| Layer | Between | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **SLA** — service level agreement | AgentCo → human | "You'll be told within 15 minutes if delivery stops." |
| **Customer responsibilities** | Human → AgentCo | "Urgent decisions answered within 4 business hours." |
| **OLA** — operational level agreement | Component ↔ component inside AgentCo | "Security gate result within 30 minutes of CI green." |
| **Underpinning dependency** | Suppliers → AgentCo | Anthropic, DeepSeek/OpenCode availability. There are no negotiated SLAs, so these are *observed* figures, not promises (§9). |

Every service level has an **SLI** (a measurement), an **SLO** (a target), and a data source. All SLIs are computed deterministically from the engine event log and audit log (core engine design §11), never estimated by an agent.

### 1.3 Conversation vs. operational alerts

The escalation design says only Opus reaches the human. This document adds a second, deliberately narrow channel, and the distinction needs to be exact:

- **Conversation:** anything an agent wants the human to know, decide, or read. It flows *only* through `human_relay` → Client Comms (T3) → monitor inbox. Unchanged.
- **Operational alerts:** templated, factual notices generated by the **driver** (not an agent, no LLM) about the *state of the system*: "T3 unavailable since 14:02; 3 decisions queued," "Delivery stopped: all ready work blocked," "Security incident: outbound prompt blocked." They carry no agent-authored content beyond titles already written by Client Comms.

The alert channel exists because the conversation channel can itself fail (§4.3). Without it, a T3 outage would be invisible to the one person who can act on it.

## 2. Service levels

Values are **proposed defaults**, stored in `config/service_levels.yaml` (a governance CI). They should be recalibrated after Phase 1 benchmarks (core engine design §17).

### 2.1 SLA — what AgentCo promises the human

| # | Service | SLI | SLO |
|---|---|---|---|
| SLA-1 | S4 | Time from delivery stopping (engine `idle_blocked`, tier outage affecting ready work, kill switch tripped by a rule) to operational alert | ≤ 15 min, any hour |
| SLA-2 | S4 | Time from Sev1 security incident detection to alert | ≤ 15 min, any hour |
| SLA-3 | S3 | Daily digest delivered | By 08:00 local on business days, 100% |
| SLA-4 | S3 | PI report delivered after PI boundary | ≤ 4 business hours |
| SLA-5 | S2 | Human requests meeting the framing standard (§3.2: options, recommendation, default, deadline, impact) | 100% (enforced by schema) |
| SLA-6 | S2 | Human requests per PI (attention budget) | ≤ 5 per PI; overrun is itself reported in the PI report |
| SLA-7 | S3 | Delivery forecast accuracy: share of features finishing on or before their P85 forecast date | ≥ 80% per rolling 2 PIs |
| SLA-8 | S1/S4 | Paid-tier spend within budget guardrails | 100% (hard control, core engine design §13) |
| SLA-9 | S3 | Spend forecast accuracy per PI | Within ±20% |
| SLA-10 | S1 | Critical vulnerability in a product under development remediated | §10 table |

### 2.2 Customer responsibilities — what the human is asked for

These aren't obligations the system can enforce. They're **expectations the system plans around**, and §3 defines exactly what happens when one isn't met.

| Urgency | Assigned when | Requested response |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Blocks > 50% of ready work, or security-related, or a production deployment is waiting | 4 business hours |
| **P2** | Blocks some ready work, or blocks a feature on the current PI's critical path | 1 business day |
| **P3** | Blocks nothing now; affects future planning | 3 business days |

Urgency is **computed by the engine** from the dependency graph and forecast (share of ready work blocked, forecast delay per day waiting), not declared by an agent. Business hours, time zone, and quiet hours are in `service_levels.yaml`. Only SLA-1/SLA-2 alerts break quiet hours.

### 2.3 OLAs — internal targets

| OLA | SLI | Target (p90) |
|---|---|---|
| Dispatch | Time from `ready` + resources free to job leased | ≤ 30 s |
| T0 job | Lease → returned | ≤ 5 min |
| T1 job | Lease → returned | ≤ 30 min |
| T2 / T3 job | Lease → returned | ≤ 3 min |
| Decomposition | Story `ready` → its tasks `ready` | ≤ 1 h |
| Referral turnaround | Referral created → answer published | ≤ 1 h |
| Security gate | CI green → gate result | ≤ 30 min |
| Exec Dir decision | Escalation arrives at T3 → resolved or `human_request` created | ≤ 1 h |
| Intake acknowledgment | Human input → templated receipt | ≤ 1 min |
| Client Comms reply | Human question ("what's the status of X?") → answer | ≤ 1 business hour |

### 2.4 SLOs for internal health

| SLO | Target | Why it's tracked |
|---|---|---|
| T0 first-pass success (complete without retry/escalation) | ≥ 70% per job type | Below this, task bounds are too coarse (escalation design §8) |
| Schema-valid output rate per model | ≥ 97% | Model/prompt fit regression signal |
| T1 resolution rate | ≥ 20% | Below this, T1 isn't earning its wall-clock cost (escalation design §8) |
| Local endpoint availability | ≥ 99% during business hours | Underpins S1 |
| Dead-letter rate | ≤ 1% of jobs | Poison work / systemic failure signal |

### 2.5 Error budget policy

Each SLO has an error budget per PI (the allowed miss). When a budget is exhausted:
- Non-essential **system changes** touching that area are frozen, except fixes (change/config design §4.2).
- A reliability **enabler** is created at the top of the next PI's backlog.
- The PI report states which budget ran out and what was done.

This prevents repeated tuning changes on top of an already unstable area, and makes reliability work compete for priority on the record rather than being done informally.

## 3. When the human doesn't answer

### 3.1 Principle

An unanswered question must never stall the whole company, and must never be silently guessed. The engine already parks only the affected items and keeps everything else moving (core engine design §9, §15). This section defines what happens to the parked items over time.

### 3.2 The human request, as a contract

Every `human_request` is schema-validated. Client Comms can't produce a request missing any of these fields (SLA-5):

```json
{
  "request_id": "hr_0012",
  "escalation_id": "esc_9f2a",
  "trigger": "ambiguous_original_intent",
  "urgency": "P2",
  "question": "Should customers be able to check out without an account?",
  "options": [
    {"id": "a", "label": "Require an account"},
    {"id": "b", "label": "Allow guest checkout, email only"},
    {"id": "c", "label": "Allow guest checkout, no data kept"}
  ],
  "recommended": "b",
  "recommendation_rationale": "Matches the stated goal of low-friction purchase; easiest to change later.",
  "impact_of_waiting": {
    "blocked_items": 7,
    "share_of_ready_work": 0.18,
    "forecast_delay_per_day": {"feature_4": "+1.0 day"}
  },
  "default_on_expiry": {
    "action": "park",
    "reversible": true,
    "est_reversal_cost": {"iterations": 0.5, "usd": 1.80}
  },
  "deadline": "2026-09-29T17:00:00-05:00"
}
```

`impact_of_waiting` and `est_reversal_cost` come from the engine's simulation mode (core engine design §12), not from the model's guess.

### 3.3 Timeline

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| Created | Inbox item. A P1 also sends an operational alert. |
| 50% of response window | Reminder (alert channel for P1; digest line for P2/P3) |
| Deadline | **Expiry action** (§3.4); the item is marked `stale` |
| Each business day after | Listed at the top of the daily digest with its accumulated delay: "Waiting 3 days; feature_4 now forecast 3 days later" |
| 3× the response window | Exec Dir is asked to reassess. The question may be reframed, split, or narrowed; it may have become moot, or its urgency may have changed. |

### 3.4 Expiry actions

The default expiry action for everything is **park**: affected items stay `awaiting_human` and all other work continues. That is always safe.

An optional second mode, **proceed provisionally**, lets the engine act on the *recommended* option after the deadline. It's off by default and enabled per trigger type in `risk_tolerance.yaml`:

```yaml
human_request_expiry:
  never_proceed:                       # fixed: can't be enabled for these, ever
    - irreversible_external_action
    - budget_or_timeline_commitment
    - legal_or_compliance_risk_acceptance
    - production_deployment
    - governance_config_change
    - new_remote_supplier_or_data_class_exception
  allow_provisional:                   # opt-in by the human
    ambiguous_original_intent: false
    explicit_human_request_to_be_asked: false   # the human asked to be asked — never default
  max_provisional_reversal_cost:
    iterations: 1
    usd: 10.00
```

If enabled and the estimated reversal cost is under the cap:
- The recommended option is published as a canonical decision with `status: provisional` and `basis: human_default_after_expiry`.
- Every work item built on it is tagged `depends_on_provisional: hr_0012`, so the cost of a later reversal stays visible and computable.
- The provisional decision stays at the top of the inbox until answered.
- If the human later chooses differently, a superseding decision is published (data layer design §7.2). The engine creates rework for every tagged item, and the PI report shows what the provisional path cost.

## 4. Availability and degraded modes

### 4.1 Dependency map

| Capability | Local endpoint (T0/T1) | T2 supplier | T3 supplier | Driver + engine | Monitor | Host |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bounded dev/test work | ● | fallback | | ● | | ● |
| PO refinement, referrals | | ● | fallback | ● | | ● |
| Exec decisions, human framing | | | ● | ● | | ● |
| Human answers / approvals | | | | ● | ● | ● |
| Operational alerts | | | | ● | | ● (plus external watchdog, §6.4) |

### 4.2 Degraded modes

The engine exposes a single `service_mode` visible on every monitor page. Mode changes are events, and they send operational alerts per SLA-1.

| Mode | Detected by | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **normal** | — | — |
| **local_down** | Endpoint health check / circuit breaker (§4.4) | T0/T1 jobs route to T2 **only if** the data class allows it (governance design §5.2) and budget remains; otherwise they're parked. Escalation design §6.6 is amended accordingly: "escalate to T2" is subject to data class and budget, not automatic. |
| **t2_down** | Circuit breaker | Only *blocking* T2 jobs (referral answers blocking work) go to T3, within budget. Non-blocking PO refinement is parked. |
| **t3_down** | Circuit breaker | See §4.3. |
| **remote_down** (both) | Both breakers / connectivity probe | Local-only operation: bounded T0/T1 work continues; referrals and decisions park; human input is received and acknowledged by template, and queued. |
| **budget_exhausted** | Cost ledger | Behaves like `remote_down` for paid tiers, but the cause is policy, so it's reported as a decision for the human (raise the budget or wait), not as an incident. |
| **monitor_down** | Driver health check | Engine continues; human answers impossible; operational alerts still sent via the notification adapter. |
| **paused** | Kill switch | Nothing dispatches; state is preserved; resumes exactly where it stopped. |

### 4.3 T3 down: no failover of authority

Opus's authority (human contact, Exec Dir decisions) is tied to the model by design, with a runtime allow-list check (escalation design §5). **It doesn't fail over to T2.** Silently letting a weaker model act as Executive Director during an outage would break the governance model at exactly the moment nobody is watching closely.

Instead, in `t3_down`:
1. All work not requiring T3 continues.
2. T3-bound jobs (Exec Dir decisions, Client Comms framing, status report writing) queue with their age tracked.
3. The driver sends an operational alert: *"T3 (Claude Opus) unavailable since 14:02. 3 decisions and 1 status report are waiting. Delivery continues on unaffected work (62% of ready items)."*
4. The daily digest, which is deterministic and templated (§11.1), still goes out. Only the Client Comms-authored PI report waits.
5. On recovery, queued T3 jobs are dispatched in priority order, and the PI report notes the outage.

Whether the human should be able to *explicitly* delegate T3 authority to T2 for a bounded period during a long outage is left as an open question (§17). If ever added, it would be a governance config change, approved by the human, time-limited, and recorded as a risk acceptance.

### 4.4 Circuit breakers and rate limits

One circuit breaker per supplier endpoint (local server, OpenCode/DeepSeek, Anthropic):
- **Closed → open** after 5 consecutive failures or ≥ 50% failures over 2 minutes (connection errors, 5xx, timeouts).
- **Open:** no calls; the degraded mode applies.
- **Half-open** after 60 s, with exponential backoff up to 10 minutes: one probe call. Success closes the breaker.

Rate-limit responses (HTTP 429) aren't outages. They're handled by honoring the retry-after interval and reducing concurrency to that supplier, and tracked separately so a busy supplier doesn't look like a failed one. Breakers stop retry storms from burning both budget and GPU time.

## 5. Incident management

### 5.1 Definition and sources

An **incident** is an unplanned interruption, a drop in quality of a service, or a security event. Incidents are work items (`incident`, core engine design §4.4), so they get correlation IDs, timelines, and replay for free.

Sources (all automatic except the last):
- degraded-mode transitions
- drift detection (change/config design §3.2)
- SLO fast-burn alerts (§6.2)
- supervisor crash loops
- dead-lettered jobs
- a secret-scanner block on an outbound prompt (governance design §5.3)
- audit-chain verification failure
- unauthorized configuration detected
- a failed deployment smoke test
- a human report from the monitor

### 5.2 Severity

| Severity | Criteria | Automatic first response | Human notified |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sev1** | Security: secret egress attempt, audit-chain mismatch, unauthorized config running, injection that produced a gated action attempt. Or all delivery stopped. | Pause the affected scope (role, tier, or all); snapshot stores for evidence; block the offending path | Immediately, any hour |
| **Sev2** | A tier or role unavailable > 30 min; SLO fast burn; deployment rollback | Enter degraded mode; open breaker | Within 1 business hour |
| **Sev3** | Performance degradation; isolated dead letters; slow SLO burn | Log; attach to digest | Daily digest |
| **Sev4** | Cosmetic / no user impact | Log | PI report |

### 5.3 Response is deterministic; analysis may use agents

- **Response actions** (pause, revert baseline, block, open breaker, rotate-key request) are carried out only by the driver's playbooks (§12) or by the human. No agent can pause, resume, or revert anything (monitor design §4.2).
- **Analysis** may use agents. An Architect `decide` job can be asked to draft a technical root cause from the incident's event trace, and a Scrum Master job can draft a process root cause. Their output is a *draft for the post-incident review*, not an action.
- Security incidents preserve evidence: stores are snapshotted *before* any recovery step, and nothing is deleted during an open Sev1 (800-53 IR-4).

### 5.4 Closure

An incident closes when service is restored *and* a cause is recorded. Sev1 and Sev2 require a **post-incident review (PIR)** within one PI, covering timeline (auto-built from the correlation trace), cause, what detection missed, and resulting problem records or change proposals. A Sev1 PIR also triggers a review of the policies it touched (governance design §13).

## 6. Monitoring and event management

### 6.1 What's watched

| Signal | Source | Interval |
|---|---|---|
| Local endpoint health, loaded model, VRAM | Router probe | 30 s |
| Supplier health / breaker state | Router | Continuous |
| Queue depth and wait per resource | Scheduler | Continuous |
| SLIs (§2) | Event log projections | Continuous |
| Spend rate | Cost ledger | Continuous |
| Disk free (event log, model store, backups) | Driver | 5 min |
| Sandbox heartbeats | Supervisor | 15 s |
| Baseline drift | Driver | Hourly (change/config design §3.2) |
| Audit chain integrity | Compliance module | Nightly full verify; each write checks its predecessor |

### 6.2 Alerting on SLO burn

Alerts fire on **error budget burn rate**, not single bad events:
- **Fast burn:** budget consumed at ≥ 10× the sustainable rate over 1 h (confirmed over the last 5 min) → Sev2.
- **Slow burn:** ≥ 2× over 24 h → Sev3.

This catches real deterioration without paging on every failed job.

### 6.3 Alert-fatigue controls

AI 600-1 *human-AI configuration* risk applies here too: a flood of alerts trains the human to ignore them.
- Alerts deduplicate on (signal, scope) and group by correlation ID.
- At most 3 non-Sev1 operational alerts per day; the rest roll into the digest.
- Every alert says what's affected, what the system already did, and whether the human needs to act.

### 6.4 Watching the watcher

If the driver process dies, nothing inside AgentCo can send an alert. An **external watchdog** covers this: a Windows scheduled task, independent of the driver, run every 5 minutes. It checks the driver's heartbeat file and the monitor's health endpoint, and sends a fixed alert through the notification adapter if either is stale for > 10 minutes. It's the only component that monitors the driver, so it's deliberately trivial.

### 6.5 Notification adapter

This resolves the notification-delivery question from monitor design v0.1 (now monitor design §4.8): a driver-owned adapter with pluggable channels, such as a desktop toast (only useful when at the PC), email, or a self-hosted push service for phone notifications. At least one channel that reaches the human **away from the PC** is required to meet SLA-1/SLA-2. The chosen channel is a supplier decision if it's external, and alert content is Internal-class at most. Alerts carry counts and states, never task content.

## 7. Capacity and performance management

### 7.1 Resources and limits

| Resource | Limit (current) | Pressure signal | Levers |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPU (RTX 3060, 8 GB VRAM) | 1–2 concurrent local jobs; model swaps cost seconds–minutes | Queue wait p90; swap count | Model affinity batching (§7.3); T1 deprioritization; overflow to T2 |
| CPU (Ryzen 7 9700X) | Shared by llama.cpp offload and CI | Token rate drop during CI | CI slots in the scheduler (change/config design §8.3) |
| RAM (61.7 GB) | Offloaded model layers + stores | Swap activity | Fewer resident models |
| Disk | Model files (N baselines retained), event log, backups | Days-to-full projection | Retention (governance design §12), backup rotation |
| Remote rate limits | Per supplier | 429 rate | Concurrency caps per supplier |
| Budget | Guardrails | Burn vs. forecast | Tier policy |
| Human attention | Inbox WIP (core engine design §7.1) | Stale requests | Framing quality, provisional defaults |

### 7.2 Benchmark harness

`bench/`: a repeatable benchmark per local model and server-flag set. It measures prompt tok/s, generation tok/s, time to first token, cold vs. warm, at concurrency 1/2/3, and with and without CI load. Results are stored as the **performance baseline** on the model's CI record (change/config design §2).

- Re-run on every model, quantization, server, or driver change, as required change evidence.
- A throughput regression > 15% against the baseline opens a problem record.
- The first run resolves the open question on local concurrency (escalation design §9): `local_queue_overflow` and the GPU semaphore are set from measured data.

### 7.3 Model affinity

The 8 GB card can't keep all three local models resident, so every switch between `qwen36`, `deepseek9b`, and `deepseek` risks a model swap. The scheduler prefers dispatching jobs for the currently loaded model, within a bounded fairness window (default 5 minutes). Swap count is a tracked capacity metric.

### 7.4 Capacity plan

At each PI boundary, the engine's simulation mode (core engine design §12) projects the next PI's demand against capacity: GPU hours, paid-tier dollars, human requests, and disk growth. The projection goes in the PI report with the binding constraint named ("GPU-bound: T1 architect jobs will queue ~40 min at planned scope"). Hardware changes are human decisions informed by this, not triggered by it.

## 8. Backup, recovery and continuity

### 8.1 What's protected

| Data | Method | RPO | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engine event log | SQLite online backup (`VACUUM INTO`) hourly + WAL | 1 h | System of record for work; everything else can be rebuilt from it |
| Audit log | Same, plus daily chain-head anchor (governance design §12) | 1 h | Chain verified after every restore |
| Canonical records | Same | 1 h | |
| Per-agent memory stores | Nightly | 24 h | Losing a day of notes degrades agents; the raw episodic record can be rebuilt from bus history |
| CMDB, baselines, config | Git (AgentCo repo remote) | Per commit | |
| Product repos | Git remote (supplier decision, change/config design §8.1) | Per push | |
| Model files | Second local disk copy, hash-verified | n/a | Re-downloadable, but pinned hashes may not be available upstream forever |

Never copy live SQLite files at the filesystem level; always use the online backup API.

### 8.2 Offsite and encryption

Backups are Confidential-class. The offsite copy (external drive rotated off-site, or cloud storage as a supplier decision) is encrypted at rest. The key is held by the human, not the driver, so a compromised host can't also decrypt its offsite history. Key custody is recorded in the runbook (§12).

### 8.3 Recovery targets

| Scenario | RTO | Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| Driver/engine crash | ≤ 15 min | Automatic restart; snapshot + event tail replay (core engine design §14) |
| Corrupt store | ≤ 2 h | Restore last backup; replay; verify audit chain |
| Bad system change | ≤ 15 min | Revert to previous baseline (change/config design §11) |
| Host loss | ≤ 1 business day | Rebuild from runbook: OS, drivers, pinned models (hash-verified), restore stores, verify chain, resume `paused` |

### 8.4 Restore drills

Once per PI, a restore is rehearsed into a scratch directory: restore the latest backups, replay the event log, verify the audit chain, and compare the projected board state to live. A failed drill is a Sev2 incident, because an untested backup provides no real assurance (800-53 CP-4).

## 9. Supplier operations

The governance design covers *whether* to use a supplier (§8.2 of that doc). This section covers *how they're performing*:

- **Per supplier:** observed availability, latency p50/p90, error and 429 rates, cost per call trend, provider model-ID changes (change/config design §3.3).
- **Reviewed each PI** in the PI report; feeds the scheduled governance supplier review.
- **Terms monitoring:** ToS, pricing, and data-handling pages are checked quarterly by the human. A material change reopens the supplier's risk acceptance.
- **Exit plan per supplier:** what replaces it, and what it takes. Prompts use the provider-neutral worker contract, so onboarding an alternate T2 provider is a normal system change with an eval gate (change/config design §7). T3 has no drop-in substitute without revisiting the governance model, which is why T3 availability matters most (§4.3).

## 10. Vulnerability remediation (SSDF RV.2)

Findings from CI scanners, scheduled scans (governance design §9, RV.1), or the Security Reviewer become backlog items automatically, with an SLA by severity:

| Severity (CVSS v3/v4 base, or scanner equivalent) | Product under development | Product in production |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Fix item at top of queue; resolved within 2 iterations | Emergency change (change/config design §4.1); production fix needs human approval; target 48 h |
| High | Within 1 PI | Within 1 week |
| Medium | Within 2 PIs | Within 1 PI |
| Low | Backlog, reviewed each PI | Backlog, reviewed each PI |

Missing an SLA is a Sev3 incident. Deferring a fix past its SLA requires a human risk acceptance with a `review_by` date (governance design §4). Repeat findings of the same class (same CWE across stories) open a problem record (RV.3, §13).

## 11. Status reporting

### 11.1 Daily digest (deterministic)

Generated by the driver from projections, with **no LLM**, so it's always correct and still arrives during a T3 outage:
- progress: items done, in progress, blocked, by feature
- pending human requests, oldest first, with accumulated delay (§3.3)
- spend today and this PI against guardrails
- service mode and any incidents
- SLO status (only those at risk)
- audit chain head hash (governance design §12)

### 11.2 PI report (Client Comms-authored, sourced)

Written by Client Communications **only from the projections and records handed to it**, never from recall (governance design §10.2, information integrity):
- per-feature status with **RAG** computed deterministically: green if P85 ≤ target date; amber if P50 ≤ target < P85; red if P50 > target
- forecast ranges (P50/P85), never single dates
- decisions made without the human, for review
- top risks from the RAID log (SAFe/PMO design, to follow)
- spend vs. budget and next-PI cost forecast
- incidents and PIR outcomes
- SLA/SLO attainment and error budgets
- capacity outlook (§7.4)

## 12. Knowledge and runbooks

`runbooks/` in the AgentCo repo holds the human- and driver-facing operational knowledge. Agent-facing knowledge stays in `canonical_records`. Each runbook has a manual procedure and, where possible, an automated **playbook** the driver runs, with the manual steps as fallback:

| Runbook | Automated part |
|---|---|
| Local endpoint down | Breaker, degraded mode, alert |
| Supplier outage (T2 / T3) | Breaker, degraded mode, alert, queueing |
| Secret detected in outbound prompt | Block, quarantine job, snapshot, Sev1 alert; *rotation is manual* |
| Audit chain verification failure | Freeze audit writes to a side log, snapshot, Sev1 alert |
| Baseline drift | Hold affected tier/role, alert |
| Revert to previous baseline | One command from the monitor (human-initiated) |
| Restore from backup | Scripted restore + replay + verify |
| Host rebuild | Checklist; model hash verification script |
| Rotate T2/T3 credentials | Checklist; driver reload |

The **known error database** is kept next to the runbooks, fed by problem management (§13). Workarounds for known errors are applied as *configuration* (a system change), not by telling agents about them in prompts.

## 13. Problem management

A **problem** is the underlying cause of one or more incidents, or of a recurring pattern that hasn't caused an incident yet. Detection is rule-based on the event log:
- the same escalation trigger for the same `(role, job_type)` above threshold for 2 iterations
- a repeated incident signature
- a `rework_exhaustion` cluster on one component
- repeat vulnerability class (RV.3)
- forecast error beyond tolerance (SLA-7)
- a benchmark regression (§7.2)

A problem record holds symptoms, linked incidents and escalations (by correlation ID), a root-cause draft (agents may draft, §5.3), a workaround if any, and the resulting **change proposal** or **enabler**. It closes only when that change is verified (change/config design §5).

---

## 14. Continual improvement

A single **improvement register** collects every improvement idea from every source (retros, PIRs, problem records, error budget exhaustion, Inspect & Adapt, the human), so none gets lost between ceremonies. Each entry becomes either a change proposal (system) or an enabler (product), gets an owner, and is reviewed at each PI's Inspect & Adapt. Items older than 2 PIs without action are closed with a reason or escalated.

## 15. Monitor integration

## 18. Open questions / follow-ups

- **Notification channel:** which channel reaches the human away from the PC? A self-hosted push service keeps alert content on infrastructure the human controls; email is simpler but is another supplier.
- **Explicit T3 delegation during long outages** (§4.3): useful, or a dangerous escape hatch? Not in v0.1.
- **Provisional defaults** (§3.4): off everywhere by default. Whether to enable them for `ambiguous_original_intent` is best decided after seeing real stale-request data for a few PIs.
- **Default values:** every OLA and SLO in §2 is a starting guess and should be recalibrated from Phase 1 data. The first benchmark run (§7.2) replaces the capacity guesses.
- **Offsite backup destination and key custody** (§8.2): not chosen yet.
- **Business hours:** a single human's calendar is simple; if AgentCo ever serves more than one stakeholder, urgency and response windows need a per-stakeholder dimension.
